
For a 30-litre container, I'd do this:

Add about 120–150 mm (5–6 inches) of compost to the bottom.
Place 2 Maris Peer seed potatoes on top, opposite each other.
Cover them with 100–120 mm (4–5 inches) of compost.
As the shoots grow to about 15 cm (6 inches) tall, add more compost until only the top few centimetres of foliage are showing.
Repeat until the container is full.

The final compost level will end up just a couple of centimetres below the rim so there's room for watering.

Watering
Keep the compost just moist until the shoots appear.
Once the foliage is growing strongly, water deeply whenever the top few centimetres begin to dry out.
Containers dry out quickly, especially once the plants are large, so they may need watering every day in hot weather.
Feeding

Once the plants are about 20–30 cm tall, start feeding every 1–2 weeks with a high-potash fertiliser, such as tomato feed.

Since you're getting your allotment going as well, these container-grown Maris Peer will give you a nice early crop while your other vegetables are establishing. Harvest them when the foliage starts to yellow, or if you're impatient, gently feel around for a few "new potatoes" once they've finished flowering.
